# Business data template guide

Use samples/business_demo.xlsx. Its five worksheets represent different parts of the business and must be imported together. The first row contains English column names; do not add a title row. The source field only identifies the synthetic sample source and does not affect pricing. The sample is neither a research report nor real company data.

## Products

The existing columns sku, name_zh, name_en, name_ms, brand, spec, unit, unit_price, and aliases remain supported. The new mode also requires category and a positive unit_cost for category matching and estimated gross margin; applications and preferences are optional.

- category: examples include cement, pvc_pipe, paint, and nails.
- applications: separate values with a vertical bar, such as plastering|masonry. Common Chinese, English, and Malay terms for plastering, masonry, concrete, indoor, and outdoor use are supported.
- preferences: fast_setting, waterproof, and low_odour; combine them with vertical bars.
- unit_cost: product cost per unit in MYR, with at most two decimal places. Unit price and cost must be positive numbers, not formulas.
- aliases: separate product aliases with vertical bars. Do not treat intended-use tags as package specifications.

SKUs must be unique. At least one of the three language-specific product names is required; providing all three is recommended. Units are bag / piece / roll / kg. Products supports up to 2000 products; the entire file must be no larger than 5MB. Existing basic price lists remain usable, but do not automatically provide business data.

## Inventory

sku, stock, low_stock, high_stock. Include exactly one row per product; every SKU must exist in Products. stock may be 0, and high_stock must exceed low_stock. Count-based products require integer quantities; kg allows three decimal places.

This is an inventory snapshot. Quotations and customer outcome records do not automatically receive, issue, or reserve stock. Update the Excel workbook after actual inventory changes, then import it again. Quantities for the same SKU across multiple quotation rows are combined for the stock check; splitting rows cannot bypass inventory limits.

## History

order_id, date, sku, quantity, area, discount_pct, delivery_cost, delivery_fee, outcome.

- Each row represents one SKU in a historical order. An order with several products may repeat order_id, but the order_id + sku combination must be unique.
- The date, area, discount, delivery cost, delivery fee charged, and outcome must be identical across rows belonging to the same order.
- date: YYYY-MM-DD or an Excel date. Future records are not accepted.
- quantity: the quantity ordered for this SKU. Enter 3 in discount_pct for 3%, rather than 0.03.
- delivery_cost: actual delivery cost. delivery_fee: the delivery fee actually charged to the customer. Do not confuse them.
- outcome: won, lost, or open. Only won/lost orders enter the conversion-rate denominator; average discounts and sales volume use won orders.
- Delivery history uses only actual delivery costs from won orders in that area. Multiple rows in one order contribute only one delivery cost.

The history sheet may contain only its header. In that case, the system reports insufficient samples and defaults to no discount. The limit is 10000 rows. An import is treated as a complete snapshot of the company's current historical data; importing the same workbook again does not append duplicate orders. Customer outcomes recorded in the application are stored separately and deduplicated by Quote ID and the latest evaluated revision.

## Delivery

area, aliases, base_fee, cost. area is the official area name; aliases contains alternative area names separated by vertical bars. base_fee is the base charge, and cost is the reference delivery cost when actual historical data is unavailable.

Example: Shah Alam / 莎阿南|沙阿南|ShahAlam / 35 / 30. The sample historical average actual delivery cost for this area is RM28. With sufficient samples and high stock, a subsidy of RM3 is subtracted, giving a recommended delivery fee of RM25.

Area aliases must not overlap. Unknown delivery areas require the owner to add data. Staff may instead explicitly select pickup, which has a delivery fee of 0.

## Rules

key, value. Only the following rule names are allowed, with one row per key:

| key | Sample value | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| min_margin_pct | 15 | Minimum gross margin percentage based on estimated costs |
| max_discount_pct | 10 | Maximum discount percentage |
| max_delivery_subsidy | 3 | Maximum delivery subsidy in MYR |
| minimum_similar_orders | 3 | Minimum number of similar closed orders |
| sales_window_days | 90 | Number of days used to observe recent won sales |

Minimum estimated gross margin is calculated as (final revenue − product cost − estimated delivery cost) / final revenue. The backend retains Decimal calculations and two-decimal-place monetary values. The rules do not guarantee actual profit; delivery costs may still change.

## Replacing the sample with real company data

Edit all five sheets in a copy, keep their relationships consistent, then check and import the workbook on the business data page. A new import creates a new version without changing prices in old approvals. Older recommendations that have not been approved require review against the new version. Do not combine a new product price list with old inventory or rules from a different workbook; the system prompts you to import a matching, complete business workbook first.
